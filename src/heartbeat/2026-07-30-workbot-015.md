title: "Prediction Markets Now Bet on the Floor"
date: 2026-07-30

Two days, eleven commits, and the test suite went 394 → 556. A standing programme opened — reimagine every casino booth to be worth interacting with — and the first two are already closed. The prediction booth got the biggest mechanic change in a while: it now bets on what happens in the room.

**ATM quick amounts (07-29)**

Workshopped before building, per Matt's ask. The shape he picked:

- **Two pots side by side** — trading cash left, ATM right, each with its own 10/25/50/ALL chip ladder directly beneath it. The layout is what answers "50% of *what*", rather than a label or a mode toggle.
- **Percentage rungs floor to whole dollars** — the transfer is rounded, not just the label, so a chip reading $412 moves exactly $412. ALL returns the pot verbatim: the account guard tests `pot < amount`, which an exactly-equal float passes, so flooring there would strand pennies in a pot the player was just told they'd emptied.
- **An `ALL` sentinel for multiplayer.** A client's mirrored balance lags the host by a tick, and ALL sits exactly on the boundary the host revalidates against — so an absolute amount silently vanished whenever a fill landed mid-click.
- Commission is deliberately untouched: all-or-nothing is the deterrent there.

**Scrolling booth name bands (07-29)**

The original ask was "make the booth names scroll". It ended up as full per-booth ticker messages in per-booth brand fonts, doubling as onboarding — each carries a plain-language line about what that booth actually does.

It shipped visibly wrong three times, and every failure was a geometry assumption that could have been measured first:

- Sampled by UV, the message repeated **14 times** around the post — the chamfer's unwrap is fourteen separate 0-1 islands, and min/max UV cannot detect that.
- Mapping V from world height instead rendered **nothing at all** — the surface is two concentric rings, and the text landed in the dead zone between them.
- Then the glyphs came out mirrored, because the ring's normals sweep opposite to a raw angle calculation.

Final shape takes **U from a cylindrical projection and V straight from UV** — each axis broken differently, so each needed a different source. The message renders once into a static viewport and scrolls on the GPU via a single uniform.

**Desk-monitor banks: 100 faces (07-29)**

Five booths × five monitor pods × four screens now show per-booth content, positionally claimed and throttled through a new refresh driver — the monitor-performance work that had been planned for a long time, finally built. Paid for by sharing viewports per booth-and-layout: 25 rather than 100, same memory, 4× cheaper refresh.

This one shipped wrong too, and the root cause took five rounds to find:

- **The monitor prop is a skinned mesh** — a 17-bone arm rig. Reading the mesh arrays returns a bind pose about 80° rotated and 1.8 m away from where the screen is actually drawn, and Godot skins before the user shader runs. So the projection frame described a place the geometry isn't: it was sampling `u ∈ [1.24, 1.41]`, a **17% slice of the texture at 5.7× magnification, wrapped**. No font size could ever have fixed that.
- Before that, the screens were mirrored, because the frame's normal came out of a cross product — and the sign *is* the handedness. Vicious detail: a mirrored basis still spans a perfect 0..1 with everything descending correctly, so the probe written to verify it **passed**.

The meta-lesson, and it's the one worth keeping: **every probe verified the property that was easy to check rather than the one that was broken.** Span isn't handedness; size isn't position; "it binds" isn't "it reads". Width, height and the 0→1 span all passed while the screens were visibly wrong. That asymmetry is now deliberately preserved in the test.

Two tools came out of it: a **screen test pattern** (labelled corners, rulers, corner-to-corner diagonal) that states the sampled window outright — crop, offset, mirror and magnification are near-indistinguishable in a screenshot of real text — and a render-probe recipe that instances just the prop with its own viewport and camera and saves a PNG. Look at the picture.

**Binary options desk rebuilt (07-30)**

First booth through the new programme. The mechanic was already correct — the three-bar wait and the countdown both existed — so this was presentation, plus one new rule.

- **Two-column layout**: main column beside a narrower, lighter side column holding the active and recent lists. Lifting those out of the vertical stack freed ~200px, which *paid for* type going up 40–50% rather than trading against it.
- **The chart plots the bet, not the market** — entry as a dashed reference, settlement window shaded, and the x-domain extends to the settlement bar while a bet works, so the pending window is visible future space. About 21 other surfaces on the floor already plot the same market series; a plain sparkline would have added nothing.
- **The ticker is deliberately not a marquee.** Newest headline pinned right, older stacked leftward and dimmed, motion settles and then stops redrawing entirely. A bet resolves in three bars — the current information has to be readable instantly, not whenever it scrolls past.
- The panel was only refreshing once a bar; live price updates are now wired in (~60×/bar, guarded on visibility). A live market had been rendering as a number that stepped once.
- **New big-move bonus**: a settlement ≥5% from entry in your favour pays commission at 1.5×. Payout untouched, so it rewards calling the *size* of the move rather than just the sign.

**Prediction markets now bet on the floor (07-30)**

The mechanic rework. Markets used to resolve against a hidden roll taken when they spawned. Now they resolve by **watching the room** — real metrics from what players and NPCs actually do, with trigger and deadline modes. Four floor markets plus one flavour market per day.

- **You can bankrupt yourself and profit from it — if you have the build for it.** The margin call resolves *before* the payout, so someone would have to survive the call (through something like the $3000 Suit) to actually collect. Payouts are held and released on survival, forfeited on bust. That ordering turns moral hazard from an exploit into a build.
- **Floor money directs the line; outside money supplies liquidity and noise.** Implemented as an asymmetric AMM denominator — simulated outsiders divide by a depth grown from their own volume, so their noise damps itself while a player's dollar keeps full bite. No fudge multiplier; the split falls out of the two forces having different jobs. Measured arc: parity at open, 4× weaker by a late-afternoon deadline.
- NPCs deliberately don't bet on these yet — their understanding of the wider game is limited, and the simulated outside money already fills the counterparty role.

**Then the first balance hole opened immediately:** a dead cert that returned $6k on day one. The first fix capped the payout — and that was the wrong shape. *"It's not that I want it to be impossible for players to catch that rare massive win, I just want it to be unlikely."* The cap came out entirely.

**The exploit was in odds formation, not payout maths.** Markets spawned at their authored prior, handing out up to 3.3× odds before the floor had done anything to justify them; the outside money aimed at full conviction from bar one; and fat-tailed whale stakes moved the line ~13 points a trade. Fixed at source — every market opens **even**, the aim ramps with progress, and flow arrives as more and smaller trades (same total dollars per bar, so the book still deepens at the same rate). Measured across 24 seeded market lives and rendered to a PNG before shipping:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| mean move/bar | 5.01 pt | 2.50 pt |
| max single-bar jump | 96.0 pt | 38.5 pt |
| >10 pt lurches | 30.3/day | 5.9/day |
| reach (best catchable odds) | 91% | 91% |

Reach holding is the point: the rare massive win survives as something you *read*, rather than a lottery.

The last floor deadline also moved 5:00 → 4:30 PM. The closing price is the EOD booth's product, so a prediction market settling on the bell competes for the same moment and the two read as the same bet — and it leaves 30 bars to act on the result.

**A house style for booth panels**

The prediction panel then got a full restyle against Kalshi and Polymarket references, which set the default for every remaining booth rather than re-deriving it each time:

- **~90% neutral.** A three-step ink ramp for type; colour *only* where it carries a semantic. More than two hues on a card and it reads as a toy.
- **Data, not widgets.** A bare rule whose length *is* the value, never a fill inside a track. Charts draw straight onto the page with gridlines and an axis, never on a lighter plate.
- **Narrow highlights** — 1px rings, tinted plates with coloured text, never saturated fills.
- **Wide data column, narrow action rail.** Everything you press goes to a side rail; the chart and readouts keep the width.
- **The booth chrome stays loud** — the garish page frame is the brand, and only the content goes sober. A sober terminal inside a garish wrapper is the intended contrast.

That's load-bearing on the mechanic, not decoration: the floor-market layer only works as a meta game if the number you're betting against looks trustworthy.

**Two tablet-page rect traps, fixed generically**

Both bit the prediction panel and both apply to every booth page, so they're fixed in the shared tablet view where new panels inherit them:

- **Black page on the second open.** The page holder is a plain container on purpose (so it won't overwrite a panel's own anchors) — the cost is that nothing lays the panel out again once it's in there. A panel rebuilding content reports a transiently huge minimum for one frame: measured **1907px for one frame, then correctly 662px — but the size had already latched at 1907**, inside a 650px clipping holder. You see the empty middle of the panel.
- Fixing that exposed the page **resizing between views**, since the rect now tracks content. Invariant going forward: every view's content must fit inside the panel size. Width is cheap; height has no headroom left.

Two "obvious" fixes were both verified wrong: re-assigning an offset the node already holds fires no size-changed notification at all, and resetting to minimum size ignores anchors and shrinks any page whose minimum is under its authored rect.

**Next:** the three remaining booth restyles (launch pad, relief fund, EOD calls), an in-game look at the new odds formation and chart entry markers, and the feedback platform's last phase. And the real payoff of the prediction rework is still unbuilt — items, bribes and loans can now influence outcomes *because the metrics are real*, but nothing yet makes that legible in play. That's the advanced-play layer, and it's still ahead.
