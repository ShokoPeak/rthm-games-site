With a tournament date fixed, balance stops being a polish item. If the casino is easily profitable the winner is whoever exploited the booths hardest, not whoever traded well — so the data would be about the wrong thing. This pass measured before tuning throughout, and found three separate ways to get paid for nothing.

## 1. The live price wasn't live

The asset manager writes the instrument's price only when a bar *finishes*, and the next bar opens from that value. So `get_current_price()` returns **the bar's open for the whole bar** — on the host only, because clients are kept current by the candle-forming sync. Measured staleness: median 0.25%, p90 2.6%, **p99 10.7%**.

Two consequences, one of them expensive:

- **The binary desk stamped its entry price there.** So you could watch a bar run your way and then place a bet that had already won: **71.0% win rate at +31.3% EV against a 54.1% break-even.** Reading from the live price instead: 52.1% and −3.6%. The residual ~4 points is momentum, and it's left in as a skill edge.
- **The same staleness reached real fills.** The order book's market-order call *snaps* its mid to the price it's handed, so passing the stale value rewound the whole book to the bar's open rather than supplying a price. Five call sites, now routed through one helper. Tape character is preserved — bar sigma 2.117 → 2.019 on a volatile market, 0.165 → 0.170 on a quiet one — with a rendered before/after.

## 2. Predator was answering questions it couldn't answer

Measured against a live floor: **seven of the eight floor-market types could never resolve YES**, while every one opened at an enforced even 50/50. Buying NO on almost anything was free money.

The floor does 28–67 trades a day at 8 agents, its average return is negative, its best single trade reaches 0.02–0.43× the goal and it has produced **zero margin calls in every day ever measured**. The authored thresholds — 150–350 trades, +5–35% return, a full goal on one trade — sat entirely outside that.

Two traps, both of which bit:

**The threshold and the population are one problem.** Trade count scales with how many bodies are in the room, so a single absolute number cannot be right from a solo run to a 24-player LAN. The threshold now carries both a population term and a clock correction — the same accumulating metric at midday and at half four are wildly different bets.

**And fixing the opening line alone made things worse.** Markets started opening honestly, but the simulated outside money still aimed at a hardcoded 0.5 — so a market that opened at 10% got dragged back toward 50 all morning. **Measured: 17.3 points of drift at +30 bars, with the direction derivable from the opening price alone.** Every market opening above 50 drifted down, every one below drifted up. Riskless, and requiring no read of the floor whatsoever. The fix is that **the opening price and the estimator's pre-evidence anchor must be one number**. Drift 17.3 → 6.1 points, signs mixed. The residual is deliberate — a market that never moves isn't a market; what was removed is the predictability.

## Two dormant bugs the honest opening line armed

Both surfaced from day-1 screenshots: *"told I'd well surpassed the EoD target despite only earning 250 profit... it should have been game over."* Neither was reachable while every market was forced to 50/50. Opening honestly at 5%, 10%, 18% is what made them expensive.

**A resolved holding was still being marked as a live asset.** Settled rows are kept on purpose so the booth's book can show them — but the winner has already been paid into cash, and the solvency calculation counted them again. So a payout was counted twice, a *losing* bet read as no loss, and phantom collateral propped up the equity check, which is why the bust never landed. Compounded by a dictionary lookup with a default on a key that has never existed, so every holding was marked at a flat 50c. Harmless at 50/50; a **5× overstatement** on a 10c holding.

**And the market maker printed money on a round trip.** Buy and sell both filled flat at the pre-trade line and *then* moved it, so a round trip banked the impact it had just created: **$225 became $1,360 at a 5% line, and $7,437 at the clamp.** Break-even was any line under about 0.36 — which is most of them. The fix of four options on the table: **charge the average fill** — the trapezoid, since impact is linear in shares — so a flat round trip costs exactly two rakes at any line, stake, depth or side. Cheap-line bets are genuinely nerfed ($225 at 10c now buys 1,443 shares rather than 2,137) and a correct call still pays 6.4×.

## 3. An EOD cutoff that was measured, and doesn't do what it was asked to

EOD Calls got a 30-minute cutoff before close — host-guarded, greyed, with a dashed CALLS CLOSE rule drawn on the chart all day.

Then it was measured, and the honest answer is that **it removes the near-certain final quarter hour and nothing else**. The hit rate for simply calling the band the price is already in plateaus at ~52% from thirty minutes out all the way to three hours; at one minute it's 87.5%. The real cause is band width, which sits under a standing measure-don't-tune ruling, so the cutoff ships as a correct guard against the most degenerate case rather than as the fix it was hoped to be.

The tail payout also went to 20× — a display change, not an EV change: **zero tails in 126 measured days.**

## The guard that was missing

Every prior test on these markets asked whether the *arithmetic* worked. None asked whether the **question was answerable**. The new calibration test sweeps each market type's threshold band across a measured reference floor and demands both a YES and a NO outcome exist — which is exactly the property that had been false for seven of eight types for months while everything stayed green.
