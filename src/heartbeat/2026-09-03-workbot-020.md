---
title: "Two Axes, and the Drift"
date: 2026-09-03
---

The market has closed lower than it opened in most simulations for months. It was on the roadmap as a tournament blocker, and this time there was a brief — so the tape finally got opened up, with every change rendered before and after and signed off before it shipped. Along the way it turned out that four features had been silently dead, the diagnosis in the brief was backwards, and two booths had been calibrated against a bug.

## The market splits into two axes

A market now authors two independent things: a **behaviour** (where the price goes, one candle at a time) and a **path** (how it travels from that bar's open to its close). Both default to null, so the twelve markets nobody re-pointed are byte-identical.

That gives a slate rather than a single model — shelf, jump and stochastic-volatility behaviours, with stepped, spiky and glacial paths, applied to five existing markets whose fiction already fitted.

## The drift was real. The diagnosis was inverted.

The brief said gravity was pulling the price down. **Gravity was holding the price *up*, at about +31.5 basis points a bar** — disabling it closed the day at 0.161× instead of 0.864×.

The actual cause was the "stairs up, elevator down" asymmetry block being applied **unconditionally**, including at fair value where its own premise doesn't hold. Gating both multipliers on the bubble factor applies the asymmetry where it was always meant to be — above fair value — and leaves the tape even at fair value:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| mean close | 0.864 | **0.965** |
| red days | 97% | **62%** |
| green bars | 58.4% | **~50%** |

That last split is where Predator's bar-bias skew was coming from, so two problems had one cause.

**Check the direction of a force before believing it's the culprit.** The original finding had also carried a caveat that this asymmetry "may well be a large part of the drama he likes" — and it was, which is why the fix *gates* it rather than removing it.

## Four features were silently dead

The asset manager re-pinned fair value to the day's open **every bar**, reading a multiplier key that **no caller has ever supplied**. So the authored fair value applied for exactly one bar and was then overwritten, forever.

Which means four things had never once taken effect: the authored per-market fair value, every fair-value-shifting news event, player price impact, and the behaviour's own sticky drift. The pinning is gone, and the 17 authored values were zeroed to inherit the open — because every one of them had been authored blind against a number that never applied.

There's a measurement lesson attached. An older finding claimed markets opened above fair value and gravity walked them back, tracking the authored gap almost 1:1. That result **can only have come from driving the simulator directly rather than through the pipeline** — because in the actual game the authored value was being overwritten every bar. **A probe that bypasses the pipeline measures a market the player never sees.**

## Gaps, stops, and the circuit breaker

The behaviour split made discontinuity a first-class thing, which had two consequences worth stating:

- **A stop is now a trigger, not a guaranteed price.** A market that gaps through your stop no longer fills you at it.
- The chart-history generator had no gap handling at all, so a shelf market rendered its entire backdrop as a flat line.
- **The circuit breaker now clamps the intra-bar path too.** The smooth path had always walked outside it on high-volatility markets; once those samples became the printed candle, the fill price *and* the stop trigger, that pre-existing bypass had to close.

And the golden tape — the regression fixture for all of this — **had two blind spots, both found by expecting a failure and not getting one.** It settled bars without walking the frame loop, and it never passed the new behaviour and path fields, so five re-pointed markets left it green.

## Recalibrating two booths against a tape that changed

Both EOD Calls and Predator had been calibrated against the drift, and both of their own notes said to re-measure when it was fixed. Measured over **1,020 market-days** — 17 markets × 60 seeds — sampled at the four floor deadlines rather than only at the bell.

**EOD Calls.** The drift fix landed: mean close 0.862 → 0.974, days closing down 93% → 58%, and the band distribution now straddles the centre band instead of sitting entirely below it. Always calling the best band went **5.4× → 3.32×**.

The remaining edge is **structural, not tuning**, and the range dial cannot remove it. Re-cutting the ladder across its whole range: the best middle band bottoms out at 1.88×, and going narrower just moves the exploit into the 20× tails — 6.22× at the narrow end. A flat 15× across 15 bands is only fair against a uniform distribution, and closes are bell-shaped. Left where it was.

**What *was* wrong is the ladder's width per market.** It's sized from base volatility, which describes intra-*bar* movement, while the ladder is a bet on the **day** — and realised daily sigma per unit of base volatility spans **4× across the roster**. One market, a shelf behaviour with about 14 gaps a day, had a ladder covering 2.0 sigma against 5–8 for most markets: an **11.7% tail rate and a standing 2.34× EV on calling a tail, live in the shipped build.** A measured per-market correction takes the tail rate to 0.0% with the centre band unchanged.

Deriving that correction from the behaviour dials was tried and doesn't work: the tape mean-reverts, so daily sigma is nowhere near root-N times bar sigma (measured ratios 0.80 to 6.94), and gravity reverts jumps — a jump market still sits below the classic median.

**Predator.** Its bar-bias threshold ran 20–90 against a floor whose bias now sits at mean 10, p90 36 — so **four of eight stops measured 0.0% YES at every deadline.** And the same threshold was offered at four different deadlines while the underlying probability swung 7.5% → 32.1% across them. Both sides now carry a clock-only scale and a measured band, holding every stop inside 5.5–8.7 points across all four deadlines, with the lowest rung restored to the honest "more green than red?".

The calibration test's own reference floor **still held the pre-fix bias figures**, which is why it passed happily over a half-dead band. Re-measured — and its remaining weakness is now documented rather than patched: it checks the market *type*, not each threshold stop, and four reference rows cannot represent a 10%-likely outcome, so tightening it per-stop would fit the game to a weak instrument.

## The run log finally records the run

The gap was concrete: four days were played, and asked what had been seen, the log could not say. It recorded the GPU driver version and **not one fact about the run** — no market, no day, no seed.

The tournament is one-shot, so a schema that misses the floor's state means market balance, Predator's calibration and relief-claim frequency all stay unanswered for months. The logger now records run identity, the floor's full snapshot at each of the four deadlines, a per-day line with every agent's daily P&L, and a ranked best-single-day summary. Host-authoritative only — the floor snapshot already aggregates every human and bot, so **one file captures all 24 players with no new network traffic**.

**Anonymity is enforced at the logger boundary, and it had to be.** Multiplayer agent ids are built as `Net_<peer>_<Name>` — the player's typed or platform name is *inside the id*. Logging ids verbatim would have written fifty real names into a file whose own header promises it records nothing identifying, and would have looked entirely normal doing it. Every id now maps to a stable per-session alias in first-seen order, and the mapping is never written, so rows stay analysable across a run while naming nobody.

The tournament metric — best single daily P&L, raw — is tracked where the daily figure is computed, so the board, the log and the metric cannot disagree. It ships in the existing results payload, so it reaches clients for free. The known caveat is stated rather than fudged: targets compound, so raw P&L partly rewards surviving to a later day. Each day's target is in the log, so a target-relative ranking stays derivable from the same data.

Verified by driving a full day through the real floor — four snapshots at exactly the right bars, eight aliased agents, no names anywhere — and then **on the real exported executable**, where the log lands beside the binary through a branch the editor never takes.

Suite 1207/1207. Four of the six tournament blockers are now closed; the two that remain are both the fifth booth.
